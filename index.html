Tap Pool to your sheet's dice pool — the total you throw — and hold to take one back. Your Hunger tracker (0 to 5) turns that many of the pool red, exactly as the rules swap Hunger dice in: the total never changes, only how many of them are blood. The one exception is the book's: a Willpower or Humanity test takes no Hunger dice — tap a red staged die to swap it for a clean one, this roll only, without touching your tracker. Every die showing 6 or more is a success, and each pair of 10s adds two more on top. Set a difficulty and the roll calls itself. A win with a pair of 10s is a Critical, or a Messy Critical when one of those 10s is on a Hunger die. A loss with a Hunger 1 is a Bestial Failure. Leave the difficulty unset and the roll reports its successes for the table to call.

Hunger is your own state, so it sticks between rolls and through a reload. A Rouse check spends one die on its own to power a Discipline or wake for the night. It holds on a 6 or more and raises your Hunger on a 1 to 5. With Hunger at 0 it is just pass or fail. After a pool roll, Blood Surge adds your surge dice — 1 to 4, from Blood Potency, asked once and remembered (hold the button to change it) — as ordinary dice, with a Rouse check riding along: fail it and your Hunger rises for the next roll, never this one.

Each die wears a mark instead of a number. A success from 6 to 9 shows a dagger. A 10 keeps the dagger and adds sparks, or fangs when that 10 is on a Hunger die. A Hunger 1 shows a skull, and anything below 6 stays blank. The numbers they landed on sit in the line under the result and in the history.

Each Fudge die is +, −, or blank — two of each on a d6 — so it reads +1, −1, or 0. The result is the net total (−4 to +4 for four dice) plus your modifier, and it lands on the Fate ladder: Mediocre, Average, Fair, Good, Great, and up.

Six narrative dice: Ability (green), Proficiency (yellow), Boost (blue) roll toward success; Difficulty (purple), Challenge (red), Setback (grey) roll against you. Tap a chip to add that die, hold it to remove one.

Success (▲) cancels Failure (▼) and Advantage (◆) cancels Threat (✕), so the result is what is left on each axis. A Triumph (★, on the Proficiency die) is a success that also triggers something great; a Despair (its inverted twin, on the Challenge die) is a failure that goes badly wrong. Both survive cancellation.

The same narrative dice as Genesys — success/failure and advantage/threat cancel, with Triumph and Despair on the yellow and red dice — plus the white Force die. Roll two d12 — a gold Hope die and a violet Fear die — and add them. The higher die sets the tone: with Hope hands you a Hope, with Fear hands the GM a Fear. Matching dice are a Critical Success — it succeeds no matter the total, and comes with Hope. Set a difficulty and the roll resolves to success or failure.

Each advantage adds a d6 and each disadvantage subtracts one; they cancel one-for-one, and stack past one (an ally's Help adds another). Tap the buttons to add them, hold to remove. The Roll Duality button throws the Hope + Fear; the dice below it roll anything else — weapon damage and the rest.

CthulhuTech 2e is a pool of coin-flips. Roll d10 equal to your Attribute + Skill; every even die is a Hit (it glows green), every odd one misses. The engine behind Mutant, Forbidden Lands, Vaesen, and Coriolis. Roll a pool of d6 and count every 6 as a success. One success carries the roll, more is better. Tap the coloured chips to build the pool (Base, Skill, Gear), or type the notation.

Miss, and you can Push: keep the 6s and 1s and reroll the rest. The 1s left showing are banes. A Base 1 costs an attribute, a Gear 1 damages your gear. Each roll can be pushed once.

The Alien pool: Base and Skill dice, plus Stress dice equal to your Stress level. Count every 6 as a success, one carries the roll. Tap the chips to build the pool, or type the notation.

Miss, and you can Push: keep the 6s and 1s and reroll the rest. A push raises your Stress, so it adds a Stress die to the throw. A 1 on any Stress die is a Panic — look it up on the Panic table.

Blade Runner rolls two step dice: an Attribute die and a Skill die, each d6 to d12. Count successes — a 6-9 is one, a 10+ is two. One success passes, two or more is a critical. Tap a die to step it up, hold to step down. Advantage adds a third die. Disadvantage rolls only the larger.

Miss, and you can Push: reroll every die that is not already a 6 or higher. Any 1 locks and stays, and each 1 left showing costs a point of Health on a physical roll or Resolve on a mental one.

Twilight: 2000 rolls two step dice — an Attribute die and a Skill die, each d6 to d12 — and counts successes: a 6-9 is one, a 10+ is two. One success passes. Tap a die to step it up, hold to step down.

Automatic fire adds Ammo dice, a pool of d6 (tap Ammo to add one). Each 6 is an extra hit. Miss, and you can Push: reroll every die that is not a 6 or higher. Any 1 locks, and each 1 left after a push drops your weapon's Reliability.

The power roll: 2d10 plus a characteristic, read in tiers. 11 or less is tier 1, 12–16 tier 2, 17+ tier 3. A natural 19 or 20 on the dice is always tier 3 and reads as a critical — on an ability roll it also buys another main action. Edges and banes cancel one for one: one edge adds +2, one bane subtracts 2, a double edge raises the tier by one instead (a double bane lowers it). A skill that applies to a test adds +2 — fold it into the characteristic. Tap Edge or Bane to raise it, hold to lower. The Power Roll button stages the 2d10 with the current chips — it never rolls, so after a numeric damage roll one tap puts the power roll back in hand and Roll (or the tray) throws it.

The power roll: 2d10 plus a characteristic (Strength, Agility, or Mind), a relevant skill, and any circumstance bonuses — fold them all into the one modifier. 11 or less is a failure, 12–16 a partial success, 17+ a success. A natural 2–3 on the bare dice is a Doom! and a natural 19–20 a Critical! — what the Doom costs or the Critical buys is the table's call, the dice just name the event. Tap the usage die to build an item's pool instead (hold to remove one): each dungeon turn the pool is rolled and every die showing 1–2 is spent and comes off the item, the rest remain. Items live on your sheet — type or tap the pool each time. The Power Roll button stages the 2d10 back — the way home from a usage pool — and never rolls: Roll and the tray do the throwing. The Modifier is a scrub dial: tap for +1, or drag or scroll it up and down.

Implements the May 2026 alpha playtest packet. The rules will change as Crows develops, and this mode tracks that packet until updated.

The check: d20 plus a stat modifier against a DC — equal or over succeeds. The Modifier and DC chips are scrub dials: tap for one step up, or drag or scroll them. The DC runs the whole 1–30 line with the book's ladder named on the way (Easy 9, Normal 12, Hard 15, Extreme 18); scrub below 1 and it is unset — the table judges. From unset, a tap or an upward step enters at Normal 12. A natural 20 is a critical and always succeeds (an attack deals double damage dice); a natural 1 is a fumble and always fails. With advantage or disadvantage the crit reads from the kept die. Attacks (vs AC) and spellcasting (vs 10 + spell tier) are this same check — damage is ordinary dice on the strip below.

The torch burns for one hour of real time. It always stands on the tray — unlit it waits in plain ink. Tap it and the hour starts — it keeps burning across reloads and mode switches, because the clock is the rule. Its light pools on the felt in two inked tones, bright within dim. The book sheds torchlight to one near radius — the two tones are drawing, not a second rule. The tray torch and the picker button are the same control: tap either burning one to snuff it, a dead one to light fresh; the X snuffs it with the rest of the table. Under ten minutes it gutters; at zero, darkness. The torch is a clock, not a roll, so it has no notation — and it never shares to a room: your torch, your clock.

Mothership rolls 1d100 under your Stat or Save: less than the number succeeds, a fail costs 1 Stress. 00 always crits, 99 always crit-fails, 90-99 always fails, and doubles are a Critical. A Skill tier (Trained +10, Expert +15, Master +20) raises the number you roll under. Panic rolls a d20 over your Stress; roll at or under it and you Panic — look up the die on your own Panic Table. Stress is tracked here: it climbs on failed rolls and is what Panic rolls against. Damage and Wounds are ordinary dice — type 4d10 or tap the strip.

Roll d100 under your skill. At or under the skill is a Regular success; under half a Hard; under a fifth an Extreme. 01 is always a Critical; 100 always a Fumble, as does 96–100 when the skill is below 50. Bonus and penalty dice roll an extra tens die and keep the best or worst; they cancel. Set the skill on the dial, or leave it unset and the roll just reports the number.

Roll d100 under your target (a skill, or a stat ×5). 01 always succeeds and is a Critical; 100 always fails and is a Fumble. Matching digits — 11, 22, 33 … — turn a success into a Critical and a failure into a Fumble. Set the target on the dial, or leave it unset to just read the number.

Ironsworn and Starforged share one roll. Add your stat and adds to the d6 action die (the score caps at 10) and set it against two d10 challenge dice. Beat both for a Strong Hit, one for a Weak Hit, neither for a Miss. When the two challenge dice match, that is a twist — a bonus on a hit, a complication on a miss. A progress roll takes the number of filled boxes on a track (1–10) instead of the action die.

Oracles. Tap Oracles to browse and roll any table, or type one straight in: oracle: then the table name, hyphenated (oracle:action, oracle:region, oracle:50-50). The result reads out under the roll.

Oracle tables are from Ironsworn, Ironsworn: Delve, and Ironsworn: Starforged by Shawn Tomkin, used under CC BY 4.0 (the Datasworn dataset). The dice mechanics are the games' own.

A standard 52-card deck (jokers optional), drawn without replacement — the deck remembers what's gone until you Shuffle. Flip on Replace and each draw goes back in, an independent pick from whatever the deck still holds; the discard stays out of play until you shuffle. The Jokers and Replace buttons are the same as typing those words after the count — set either way, and the field always shows the deck it will draw from. Tap a drawn card to pick it up for a close look, flick it to send it to the discard without drawing, and tap the deck or table to draw; tap the discard pile to page back through it.

The courts are traced from the Great Mogul deck (Meiffre Neveu & Cie, Paris, c. 1850s), a stencilled woodcut in the single-headed English pattern; the two jokers are Fools from older tarots — Jean Noblet's (Paris, c. 1650) and a Swiss Troccas deck's. Pips, aces, and the back are original vector work. Sources and pipeline: woodcut-cards.

All 78 cards, drawn without replacement until you Shuffle. With Reversals on, each card's orientation is fixed when the deck is shuffled and a reversed card lands upside down; Majors trims the deck to the 22 trumps for a quick reading. The picker buttons and the typed words are the same thing — add majors, upright, or replace after the count, in any order, and the field always shows the deck it will draw from. Tap a drawn card to pick it up for a close look, flick it to the discard without drawing, and tap the deck or table to draw; tap the discard pile to page back through it.

The art is traced from the Jean Noblet tarot (Paris, c. 1650, Bibliothèque nationale de France) — the oldest surviving Tarot de Marseille — with the five Wands pips missing from the BnF's deck filled from the Jacques Viéville tarot (Paris, c. 1650), its contemporary across the city. Sources and pipeline: woodcut-tarot.

The 48-card Japanese flower deck — twelve months, each a flower, each holding four cards: brights (光), animals (タネ), ribbons (短冊) and chaff (カス). uta:5

uta:1 replace

The Ogura Hyakunin Isshu — one hundred poets, one poem each, compiled by Fujiwara no Teika around 1235, from Emperor Tenji to Emperor Juntoku. Draw a single card and the whole poem reads out beneath the result, with Clay MacCauley's translation. Tap a drawn card to read the calligraphy up close, flick it to the discard, tap the deck or table to draw; karuta: works as an alias.

For Bōzu Mekuri, draw one card per player in turn: a courtier keeps his card, a monk (13 in the deck) costs you your whole pile, a lady (21 in the deck) wins you everything discarded so far. Largest pile when the deck runs out wins.

The portraits and calligraphy are traced from Hishikawa Moronobu's illustrated edition (Edo, 1680) — Moronobu is often counted the first ukiyo-e artist — digitised by the Library of Congress. The deck reads in the book's own order, poet by poet, and is released as its own CC0 repository, woodcut-utagaruta.
